What a golf simulator really costs in year one, year two, year three

By David. The launch monitor price gets all the attention during shopping season. Then the renewals start arriving. I run a home sim with a Rapsodo MLM2PRO, I have paid the membership bills myself, and I built this database partly because every brand hides a piece of the total in a different place. This guide lays out the full stack: hardware, memberships, simulator software, and the room itself, across three years.

All prices below are list prices from widely published manufacturer information at time of writing. Plans change often in this category. Every figure below matches the checked data file on this site, verified October 25, 2026, and you should confirm each price on the maker page before you buy.

The four cost layers

Layer one is the launch monitor. Budget radar and camera units start near $500 to $700. Mid tier units with strong club data cluster around $2,000 to $3,000: the FlightScope Mevo+ lists at $2,299 and the Uneekor EYE MINI at $2,999. Premium floor units and overhead units run from about $5,000 into five figures.

Layer two is the manufacturer membership. This layer surprises the most buyers. Several brands gate their best features behind a yearly plan: measured spin, simulator integrations, saved session history, advanced club data. Skip the plan and the hardware still works, with a trimmed feature set that varies by brand.

Layer three is simulator software. GSPro lists at $250 per year, confirmed on gsprogolf.com. E6 Connect sells tiered yearly plans on top of its base license, $300 per year for Basic and $600 per year for Expanded at recent published prices. Some brands bundle their own software, and some include a starter library in the membership from layer two.

Layer four is the room. A net, a mat, and a side monitor can start a build under $500 in parts. A screen, a projector, and an enclosure push a tidy garage build into the $2,000 to $5,000 range. A gaming PC, if you need one, adds $800 and up as a working estimate.

Brand by brand: the traps I watch for

Rapsodo MLM2PRO

Hardware lists at $699.99. The Core plan, the tier earlier sold as Premium, lists at $199.99 per year, and it unlocks the full metric set with measured spin on RPT balls, simulator connections such as GSPro and E6, and the deeper app feature set. The free tier covers basic range practice. The trap: buyers price the unit at $700 and plan a GSPro build, then learn the GSPro connection rides on the paid Core plan. Budget $200 a year beside the GSPro fee. My own fix was the Lifetime Premium offer at $599.99 list, which Rapsodo sold during a limited window that closed in October 2026. A lifetime option turns a yearly leak into one payment, and it changes the three year math a lot when it is offered.

Garmin Approach R10

Hardware lists at $499.99, down from a $599.99 launch price in 2021. Garmin Golf membership lists at $99.99 per year. The membership carries Home Tee Hero course play and premium app features, and the core metrics and virtual range run in the app with no membership. The trap here is milder: the base app covers range sessions well, so golfers buy in, then want course play at home, and the membership joins the budget in month two. Yearly cost stays the lowest among the big portable brands, and three year totals stay friendly.

SkyTrak and SkyTrak+

SkyTrak+ sits at $1,995 right now, a clearance price with the $2,995 list struck through on SkyTrak's product page, and the model has been superseded by the ST MAX. SkyTrak sells yearly plan tiers: Essential at $99.99, Core at $299.99 with the Foresight course pack or $349.99 with the TrackMan course pack, and Elite at $599.99 with both packs. A paid plan is required to connect third party simulator software at all, and the driving range works without one. The trap has a twist here. Those paid tiers unlock connections such as E6, and GSPro has no official SkyTrak support at all. Community connector software exists, and it sits outside official support. So an E6 build carries the SkyTrak plan fee plus the E6 fee every year, and any GSPro plan needs homework before money moves. Add both lines before you compare against a brand with a single membership.

FlightScope Mevo+

Mevo+ hardware lists at $2,299, and FlightScope charges no yearly fee for the FS Golf app or the data itself. The extras are one time add ons: the Pro Package at $1,000 list, sold at $699 on flightscope.com at our October 2026 check, unlocks 11 additional club parameters, and Face Impact Location costs $499 and requires the Pro Package. The trap runs in reverse here: the add ons are one time purchases for data that other brands rent yearly. A buyer comparing sticker prices alone can miss that Mevo+ math improves in years two and three.

Uneekor and Foresight (the premium room)

Uneekor starts lower than most buyers expect: the EYE MINI floor unit lists at $2,999, and the overhead line climbs from there to the EYE XO2 at $10,999. The free Player tier covers the range, and the Pro package at $199 per year adds the third party connector that GSPro and E6 require. Foresight's GC3 lists at $6,999, with FSX 2020 and FSX Play included as perpetual licenses in current US pricing. The GSPro route on a GC3 runs through FSX Play or FSX 2020 as the bridge, or through the Foresight+ Gold plan at $599 per year for buyers without a legacy license. The trap at this tier is software stacking: hardware, brand software, and GSPro can each carry a fee. A Uneekor room renews at $449 a year in software (Pro $199 plus GSPro $250), and a GC3 room on the Gold route renews at $849 a year (Gold $599 plus GSPro $250), before you swing a club. Owners at this tier accept the stack for accuracy and convenience, and they should accept it with open eyes and a calendar reminder.

Three sample builds, three year totals

Build A: portable starter, Garmin R10

  • Year one: $499.99 hardware, $99.99 membership, net and mat setup at $400 in parts. Total near $1,000.
  • Year two: $99.99 membership. Total near $100.
  • Year three: $99.99 membership, replacement net at $120 as an estimate. Total near $220.
  • Three year total: near $1,320, range and garage ready, course play through Garmin software.

Build B: GSPro garage build, Rapsodo MLM2PRO

  • Year one: $699.99 hardware, $199.99 Core, $250 GSPro, screen and enclosure at $1,800 in parts, gaming PC already owned. Total near $2,950.
  • Year two: $199.99 Core plus $250 GSPro. Total near $450.
  • Year three: $199.99 Core plus $250 GSPro, projector bulb or lamp fund at $150 as an estimate. Total near $600.
  • Three year total: near $4,000. On my lifetime Premium deal, years two and three drop to the GSPro fee plus maintenance.

Build C: premium overhead room, Uneekor EYE XO2 class

  • Year one: EYE XO2 hardware at $10,999, Pro package at $199, $250 GSPro, finished room at $4,000 in parts and materials. Total lands near $15,450.
  • Years two and three: Pro package $199 plus GSPro $250, a software stack of $449 per year.
  • Three year total: near $16,350, with tour level data and a room that feels like a studio.

The renewal calendar trick

Write every renewal date on one page the week you set up the room. I keep mine beside the sim PC. Brands bill on their own schedules, a trial converts quietly, and a lapsed membership can lock features mid season. A single list with dates and amounts turns surprise bills into planned bills.

How to cut the total without cutting the golf

Buy hardware during the big sale windows, because this category discounts hard around major golf events and the winter holidays. Choose one simulator platform for year one and learn it fully. Share a build with your actual room: a $700 monitor in a well lit, well measured bay beats a $5,000 monitor in a cramped corner every time. Finally, price the three year total for every candidate, the way the compare pages on this site do, and let that number sit beside the sticker price in every decision.
